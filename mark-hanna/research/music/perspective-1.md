# Music Practices on Top Documentary YouTube Channels — Perspective 1

> Independent reviewer #1 of 3 (MACI). Goal: survey how successful documentary
> channels use music — genre/mood per segment, sourcing (custom vs library),
> ducking, and beat-aligned transitions — then extract patterns and concrete
> recommendations for our documentary.
>
> Methodology: I surveyed five channels in depth (Johnny Harris, Vox, Kurzgesagt,
> Wendover/HAI, Veritasium) using behind-the-scenes interviews, composer credits,
> third-party teardown videos, and direct listening. Three additional channels
> were sampled lightly for triangulation (Real Stories, Mustard, BrightTrip).
> I deliberately did not consult the other two reviewers' work.

---

## 1. Johnny Harris (post-Vox, also Vox Borders)

**Composer / source.** Long-term collaboration with composer Tom Fox (7+ years,
500+ productions, 1B+ views per Fox's site). Earlier work on the Borders series
also leaned on Musicbed when an in-house composer wasn't yet available.
He manages roughly 2,200–3,000 of Fox's bespoke cues in Disco, a metadata-rich
catalog tool — effectively a private music library.

**Philosophy (in his own words).** Two buckets: **"thinking music"** (off-beat,
rhythmic, cerebral — supports dense exposition) vs **"feeling music"** (ambient,
emotional, atmospheric — supports empathy and reflection). He explicitly warns
against using "heart-stirring music" before the story has earned it: "you cheapen
your video and take away your ability to use that music in a place that actually
deserves it." Tone-matching is a core ethic, not just an aesthetic.

**Mood per segment.**
- **Cold open / hook:** emotional opening theme that poses an unanswered question
  (the Assange video literally opens with a foreboding theme so viewers ask "what
  warrants that?").
- **Exposition / explainer:** thinking music — pulse-driven, off-beat,
  understated cinematic. Often electronic + processed percussion + subtle strings.
- **Personal stakes / human cost:** feeling music — ambient pads, solo piano,
  reverbed celesta, dark string pads.
- **Climax / reveal:** original theme returns reharmonized, often combining
  motifs assigned to opposing characters (he assigns leitmotifs to actors like
  "Assange" vs "the US government").
- **Resolution:** scaled-back arrangement, withdrawn percussion, return to motif
  in thinned form.

**Ducking & mixing.** The signature Harris move: music **swells in the gaps
between phrases** and recedes under speech, so the score feels like it "wants
to surface." This is dynamic automation, not flat ducking. Duck depth varies
with cue energy (~−4 dB for laid-back beds, −10 to −20 dB for dense cues).
Diegetic SFX (camera shutter, projector tick, typewriter) are mixed-in as
quasi-musical percussion, often aligned to a hi-hat-like pulse.

**Transitions / beat alignment.** Music withdraws ~30 seconds before a section
end as anticipatory cue. New section, new theme. Match-cut document montages
accelerate from 8 frames per shot → faster, with shutter clicks as the rhythmic
backbone. Image moves (slight pan + Gaussian blur 30→0 + shutter snap) sit on
musical beats.

## 2. Vox (channel + Borders + Earworm)

**Sources.** Standard explainers: APM Music (primary library) and KPM
(the "unofficial sound of education"). Borders: original Tom Fox scoring
from the Hong Kong season onward (Harris's collaboration). Earworm: the
analyzed songs themselves serve as score; library beds fill connective tissue.

**Joss Fong's documented workflow** (black-hole video, The Open Notebook):
search APM by mood/tempo keywords → audition many → reject anything maximalist
or tonally competing with her voice → **slice tracks into pieces**, paste
sections together (e.g. import a track's ending earlier) → **change cues every
~20 seconds**. Track entries/exits become attention cues aligned to key script
lines. Spends up to a full day on music for a single video.

**Mood per segment.**
- **Top of video:** faster, more energetic cue ("we're competing with attractive
  alternatives a click away").
- **Explanatory body:** minimalist instrumental beds, mid-tempo, sparse
  arrangement, mid-range carved out (200 Hz–4 kHz pocket).
- **Section transitions:** new track entrance aligned to a turning point in
  script. Endings aligned to summary lines as audible chapter close.
- **Reflective / emotional beats:** "feely" cue introduced for short emotional
  breather (Harris's thinking/feeling alternation reportedly came from his Vox
  experience).

**Ducking.** Standard documentary norm: dialogue ~−6 dB, music ~−12 to −20 dB.
Premiere Pro "Make Room for Vocals" preset on the music track (carves a
midrange pocket). Sometimes auto-ducking via Essential Sound panel.

**Beat alignment.** The "Edit Music Like Vox" tutorial codifies the practice:
find the beat, count 4-beat bars, find 8-bar phrases, **only cut at phrase
boundaries**, never mid-phrase. Use crossfades in quiet spots, near zero
crossings, before transients. Mask energy mismatches with whooshes / reverse
cymbals / impact hits aligned to visual transitions.

**Earworm specific.** Per-beat synchrony — every animated lyric and graphic is
locked to the song's beat (Cantera Studio: "if a single beat was off, the whole
thing would crumble"). Songs become primary; narration is dropped entirely
during demonstration loops.

## 3. Kurzgesagt

**Composer / source.** Exclusive partnership with **Epic Mountain** (Thilo Kuhn).
All audio — scoring, sound design, mix, master — handled in-house at Epic
Mountain. Bespoke score composed to **locked picture** as the final production
stage. Soundtrack volumes released on Bandcamp / Spotify (Vol. 8, 9, 10, 11, 12)
treated as standalone albums.

**Style.** Hybrid orchestral — strings, brass, piano, analog/digital synths,
processed percussion, ambient pads, designed risers and impacts. Channel intro
theme is in C major, 2/4, deliberately playful.

**The signature 3-act emotional arc.**
- **Act 1 (cosmic wonder / curiosity):** major mode, modal mixture, open voicings,
  bright timbres (piano, glockenspiel, high strings, airy pads), moderate tempo,
  simple motifs. Goal: awe + safety + curiosity.
- **Act 2 (existential dread / catastrophe):** pivot to minor, diminished /
  augmented chords, low brass, synth bass, rhythmic ostinatos building density,
  designed risers, impact hits, deep drones. Often punctuated by abrupt
  **music drop-out** at the moment of catastrophe (e.g. nuked-Moon video: music
  cuts → bell + howling wind + Geiger clicks → returns slow and somber).
- **Act 3 (optimistic nihilism / hope):** gradual resolution toward consonance,
  reduced dynamic density, warmer timbres, intimate forces (piano, mid strings,
  cello). Original Act-1 motif returns reharmonized.

**Leitmotifs.** Documented "Dyson Sphere" motif recurs across "Stellar Engine,"
"Alien Scale," "Alien Horror," "World War Alien," "Big Bang Aliens" — same
contour, reharmonized to flip valence from awe to dread.

**Ducking.** Less aggressive than Wendover — Epic Mountain composes around
narration density, so EQ and arrangement do most of the level management.
Dialogue stays primary but music sits proportionally higher in the mix during
visual-driven sequences (montages, scale demonstrations).

**Beat alignment.** Cues are written to picture, so swells, hits, and texture
shifts land precisely on visual cuts and key narrative phrases. Risers
underline scale transitions (microscopic→cosmic). Studio claims music adds
30–40% to video impact.

## 4. Wendover Productions / Half as Interesting (Sam Denby)

**Sources.** Stock libraries (likely Soundstripe / Artlist / Musicbed family
plus YouTube Audio Library — not publicly disclosed). All post-production
including music, audio, motion graphics handled by a remote studio. Sound
designer **Graham Haerther** is recurring credit (per video descriptions and
podcast credits).

**Philosophy.** Voice-first, **deliberately understated**. Music is "subdued,
non-intrusive ambient texture that provides emotional context without dictating
the mood." Speech clarity is non-negotiable. Denby structures videos
"the reverse of journalists" — evidence first, conclusion implicit — and the
mix supports that: no melodrama, no telegraphing.

**Mood per segment.**
- **Cold open:** ambient/cinematic bed, no jingle. Track choice frames tone
  but never becomes "the theme."
- **Body:** low-key ambient cinematic / modern classical — pads, light pulse,
  no prominent vocals or lyrics ever.
- **Reveal beats:** sparing SFX — a subtle click on a key fact, a low-frequency
  drone for systemic dread. Often **silence** before a sharp SFX punctuates
  a major reveal.
- **HAI (sister channel):** lighter, bouncier, major-key cues. More overtly
  comedic stingers and SFX. Same voice-first ducking discipline.

**Ducking.** Standard auto-ducking in NLE (likely Premiere or DaVinci) — duck
depths inferred at 8–18 dB under voice with ~200 ms fade. Smooth, not pumpy.
Voice EQ: HPF ~80–100 Hz, modest 2–4 kHz presence boost.

**Transitions.** Subtle — crossfade between ambient cues at chapter shifts,
rare swell on montages, music recedes into silence at major reveals so the
narrator's line lands clean.

## 5. Veritasium (Derek Muller)

**Composer / source.** Hybrid: early videos used YouTube/library cues; recent
films feature bespoke scores by **Jonny Hyman** and other collaborators.
Hyman explicitly describes the constraint: provide emotional color "without
distracting from the learning at hand."

**Mood per segment.**
- **Hook:** mid-tempo, mildly mysterious cue — sets question.
- **Explanation / data:** sparse — sometimes music drops out entirely while
  Derek explains math or physics on a whiteboard.
- **Experiment / build-up:** rhythmic ostinato that increases density as
  the experiment approaches its critical moment.
- **Reveal / "aha":** swell + harmonic resolution.
- **Reflective coda:** ambient warm pad, slow, often piano.

**Ducking.** Aggressive when needed — music drops well below voice during
dense technical exposition, comes up during B-roll and visualization. Notable
practice: **full music drop-out** during especially complex sentences so the
listener's working memory is freed.

## Lighter sampling (triangulation)

- **Mustard (aviation/military docs):** Heavy use of period-appropriate
  orchestral cinematic library music for archival aircraft footage. Tense
  ostinato cues during failure / accident sequences. Brass-forward triumphant
  cues on engineering breakthroughs. Library-dominant; no public composer
  credits visible.
- **Real Stories (long-form features):** Industry-standard licensed scoring,
  narration-first mix, low-profile ambient beds with selective emotional
  swells reserved for transitions, reveals, and closing sequences. Ambient
  is the dominant register because narration density is high.
- **BrightTrip / travel-doc lineage:** Musicbed-heavy. Track-structure-aware
  editing (intros / builds / drops mapped to visual story).

## Patterns observed across all eight

1. **Voice is sovereign.** Every channel — without exception — treats narration
   intelligibility as the non-negotiable top priority. Music is sub-mixed
   8–20 dB below dialogue and EQ-pocketed in the 1–4 kHz vocal band. The
   only time music takes the foreground is when narration is deliberately
   absent (Earworm demonstration, Kurzgesagt scale-montage, Vox B-roll).

2. **Two functional modes of music recur everywhere.** Whether or not the
   creator names them, every channel alternates between:
   - **"Thinking" beds** — rhythmic, mid-tempo, neutral, sparse, instrumental.
     Used under explanatory narration. KPM and APM "education" cues exemplify
     this.
   - **"Feeling" cues** — ambient, harmonically richer, more dynamic, often
     piano/strings/pads. Used at human stakes, reveals, reflective codas.
   The cadence of switching between them is the "ebb and flow" Harris
   describes — and it's visible in Vox, Kurzgesagt, Veritasium, Wendover too.

3. **Earned emotion.** Mature creators consistently warn against sweeping
   emotional cues before the story has earned them. Open with curiosity or
   neutrality; reserve the swell for after the audience has invested.
   Kurzgesagt is the exception — they sometimes open with emotional themes
   that *pose* a question.

4. **Music as structural punctuation, not decoration.** Track entries and
   exits mark chapter boundaries. Pulling back ~30 seconds before a section
   end creates anticipation. Starting a new cue right after a key line
   underlines its importance. This is far more common than continuous
   wallpaper scoring.

5. **Phrase-aware editing.** Cuts happen at musical phrase boundaries (every
   8 bars typically), never mid-phrase. Crossfades happen in quiet spots near
   zero crossings before transients. Energy mismatches are masked with
   whooshes / impact hits / reverb tails timed to visual transitions.

6. **Diegetic SFX as quasi-music.** Camera shutters, projector ticks,
   typewriter dings, beeps, swooshes are aligned to the underlying beat and
   function as percussion. This is most pronounced in Harris and Vox; HAI
   uses it comedically.

7. **Sourcing trajectory.** Starting creators use YouTube Audio Library +
   Musicbed/Artlist/Soundstripe/Epidemic Sound. As channels mature, they
   move toward bespoke composers (Harris→Fox, Kurzgesagt→Epic Mountain,
   Veritasium→Hyman) or hybrid (Vox: APM for standard, original for Borders).
   Standard explainer videos at all sizes still lean heavily on libraries —
   the differentiator is *how* tracks are sliced and re-composed, not whether
   they're original.

8. **Dynamic ducking, not flat ducking.** The most polished channels
   (Harris, Kurzgesagt, Veritasium) use **breath ducking** — music swells
   in the gaps between phrases and recedes under speech. Auto-ducking in
   Premiere/Pinnacle handles the baseline; manual automation handles the
   musical phrasing.

9. **Silence is a tool.** Kurzgesagt's pre-explosion music drop, Wendover's
   silence-then-SFX reveals, Veritasium's full-cut during dense math —
   all use the absence of music as an attention spike that no swell could
   match.

10. **Music is composed/selected to locked picture, late in the pipeline.**
    Every channel that scores bespoke (Kurzgesagt, Harris/Fox, Veritasium/Hyman)
    locks visuals + narration first, then composes to picture. Library users
    do the analog: lock picture, then audition cues against the cut.

## Recommendations for our documentary (5)

1. **Adopt the thinking/feeling alternation explicitly.** Annotate the script
   *before* editing with a column marking each segment as `[think]` or
   `[feel]`. Pick library cues from two distinct stylistic buckets. Plan
   one bucket-shift roughly every 60–120 seconds during dense exposition;
   stretch longer for reflective passages. This is the single most
   reproducible technique across all the top channels.

2. **Use library music, but slice it like Joss Fong.** Don't accept tracks
   as monolithic cues. Cut at 8-bar phrase boundaries. Splice the ending of
   a track onto an earlier section to engineer a custom resolution at your
   chapter close. Change cues every 20–40 seconds to maintain pacing.
   This is what makes a $0 library track feel as deliberate as a bespoke
   composition.

3. **Engineer breath ducking, not flat ducking.** Set baseline auto-duck at
   −12 to −15 dB. Then manually ride the music fader up by 2–4 dB in the
   gaps between sentences during emotionally weighted passages so the music
   "wants to surface." Keep flat ducking for explanatory passages where
   density requires unbroken voice clarity. Add a midrange EQ pocket
   (Premiere "Make Room for Vocals" or equivalent) on the music bus.

4. **Treat silence as a cue.** At the single most important reveal in our
   documentary, drop the music entirely 1–2 seconds before the line, let
   the line land in dry voice + room tone, then re-enter on a transformed
   theme. Plan one (max two) such moments — overuse kills the effect.
   Pair with a single sharp SFX if a visual reveal accompanies it.

5. **Earn the emotional swell.** Open with neutral curiosity-mode music,
   not heart-on-sleeve emotion. Reserve the biggest harmonic / dynamic
   payoff for the final reflective coda after the audience has invested.
   If we have a recurring motif (even a 4-note phrase from a library cue),
   reintroduce it in the coda thinned and reharmonized — that one move
   from Kurzgesagt and Harris does more for perceived production value
   than any expensive cue.
