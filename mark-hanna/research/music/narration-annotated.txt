SCENE 2 — WHO WAS  (53.01 – 119.96s, lines 27-69)
================================================================================

[mechanism]                     Lines 27-31 — Cue A re-enters quietly under the origin setup; this is the structural backstory of the man-who-became-the-machine.
27| He wasn't born with money. He was born in a grocery.
29| New Lisbon, Ohio. Eighteen thirty-seven.
31| His father, Doctor Leonard Hanna, was a country physician who ran a wholesale grocery on the side. Flour, salt, dry goods to Ohio farmers.

[mechanism]                     Lines 33-39 — Cleveland + Western Reserve + Rockefeller foreshadow. Still Cue A.
33| When Mark was fifteen, the family moved to Cleveland. He enrolled at Western Reserve College.
35| In his class, was a quiet, awkward boy who would grow up to control ninety percent of the oil refining capacity in the United States.
37| His name was John D. Rockefeller.
39| The two would orbit each other for the next forty years.

[mechanism]                     Lines 41-43 — Hanna's actual education (the price of things). Cue A continuing forward motion.
41| Hanna lasted one year at Western Reserve before he quit. Not because he was failing. Because he had already figured out something the professors couldn't teach. The real education, was in the price of things.
43| He went back to the store. Worked the floor. Watched how men negotiated. Watched who paid on time and who didn't. Watched which suppliers had leverage, and which ones begged.

[weight]                        Lines 45-49 — Cue B floats in: civil war, marriage, father-in-law's coal-and-iron money. Persons named, brief swap.
45| Then the Civil War came. He served briefly. Came home.
47| And married a woman named Charlotte Rhodes.
49| Her father was Daniel Rhodes. Coal and iron. One of the men quietly getting rich off everything Cleveland was becoming.

[mechanism]                     Lines 51-57 — Cue A returns: building MA Hanna & Co., the Cleveland chokepoint, the cut on every ton of ore.
51| Hanna walked into that business, and never walked out.
53| By eighteen sixty-seven, Marcus Alonzo Hanna had built MA Hanna and Company. A coal and iron brokerage, sitting at the exact pivot point where Lake Erie ore boats unloaded for the Pittsburgh steel mills.
55| Cleveland was the chokepoint. And Hanna sat on it.
57| Every ton of iron ore that crossed Lake Erie in the eighteen seventies, passed through brokers like him, he took a cut.

[mechanism]                     Lines 59-61 — empire expansion. Cue A still on; this is the last mechanism beat before the philosophical pivot.
59| Then he bought the newspaper, the Cleveland Herald. Then a theater. Then banking interests. Then a streetcar line.

[weight]                        Line 63 — Cue B brief: his interior state (boredom is the human pivot to ambition). Cue A exits here.

[mechanism]                     Line 65 — final mechanism beat sets up the [dry]. Cue B should also be out by mid-line; bed is sparse.

================================================================================

[mechanism]                     Lines 71-77 — Cue A enters firmly: 1888 RNC, kingmaker, Sherman, working the floor, trading delegates. This is the machinery in motion.

73| Mark Hanna arrived not as a candidate, but as a kingmaker.
75| His man was John Sherman. Ohio senator. Treasury veteran. The establishment choice.

[weight]                        Line 79 — Cue B: the count, the bleed-out. Vote figures = §3.2 weight trigger.

[silent-reveal] *** THE ONE FOR THE WHOLE PIECE *** Line 81 — full music drop 1-2s BEFORE this line; dry voice + room tone; re-enter ~1.5s after on a single low piano note (transformed Cue A) per PLAN §3.3.
81| Hanna lost.

  Reasoning for picking line 81 as the silent-reveal:
   • Two words. Maximum density-per-second when the bed is gone.
   • Total narrative reversal — the man we've watched build a machine for 40 lines just lost. The structural inversion is the whole reason the next reveal (McKinley) lands.
   • Falls roughly 60% into runtime (~135s of 238s) — the canonical mid-act-3 silence position in P1's reference cuts.
   • Re-entry on a single low piano note (transformed Cue A, per §3.3) is exactly what the cut needs to start the McKinley arc on motion-not-mood.
