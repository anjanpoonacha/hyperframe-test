# Documentary Music Plan

> Synthesis of three independent reviewer perspectives (MACI triangulation).
> Confidence is derived from agreement across reviewers, not self-reported.
>
> - **Perspective 1** (mainstream/explainer axis): Johnny Harris, Vox, Kurzgesagt, Wendover/HAI, Veritasium + 3 lighter samples. Library-driven craft, "thinking vs feeling" alternation, breath ducking, slice library tracks at 8-bar phrase boundaries.
> - **Perspective 2** (indie/auteur axis): LEMMiNO, Defunctland, Internet Historian, Mustard, Plainly Difficult + 3 contrast points. Two-tier sourcing rule (commission OR commit to one library; avoid the middle), 4–8 cues per 15–30 min, silence ≥0.5 s before reveals, ducking wider than tutorials say (−5 to −8 dB), restraint reads as honesty.
> - **Perspective 3** (independent position paper, project-anchored): explicit assumption of the short-form Mark Hanna / 1896 historical doc context. Strict minimalist: 40–60% of runtime unscored, two leitmotifs only ("motion" + "weight"), period-PD → original/AI → named CC-BY sourcing order, binary copyright stance, score structure not timecodes.

This plan is for the **Mark Hanna / 1896 historical documentary**. P3's project-specific context (`hyperframe-test/mark-hanna/`, short-form, captions-on, ad-monetized YouTube, zero cash budget) is treated as the canonical project description; P1 and P2 are filtered through that lens because their channel surveys come from a wider register (long-form explainers and indie features). Where P1/P2 recommend something the Mark Hanna project shouldn't adopt, that disagreement is recorded explicitly rather than silently averaged.

**Goal:** produce an actionable, prescriptive music plan for a short-form 1896 historical documentary on a zero-cash budget, that resolves three independent reviewers' disagreements via project context (DESIGN.md visual brief, narration runtime, captions-on YouTube ad-monetized distribution) rather than by averaging.

## 1. Points of agreement (high-confidence — adopt as defaults)

These five rules are stated explicitly in **all three** independent reports. Treat them as the core.

### 1.1 Silence is structural, not absence
All three reviewers identify silence/music drop-out as the single highest-leverage move on a serious documentary.
- P1: drop the music entirely 1–2 s before the line at the single most important reveal; let it land in dry voice + room tone; re-enter on a transformed theme.
- P2: ≥0.5 s of full silence before key reveals is consistent enough across LEMMiNO, Defunctland's emotional features, and Plainly Difficult to call it a rule.
- P3: score from silence outward; aim for 40–60% of runtime unscored; the most important factual claim in each section plays dry.

**Rule:** plan one (max two) "silent reveal" moments in the locked cut and score around them. The single most important sentence in each section plays dry.

### 1.2 Motif reuse beats cue variety
- P1: "earn the emotional swell" — reintroduce a recurring motif in the coda, thinned and reharmonized.
- P2: 4–8 cues for a 15–30 min video; "reuse the opening cue's melody as the closing cue's harmony" (Mustard's craft move).
- P3: exactly two leitmotifs ("motion" + "weight"), reused with intent.

**Rule:** the documentary has a finite, named cue palette. By the third recurrence the audience builds an associative shortcut and the cue does narrative work for free.

### 1.3 Score function, not timecode
- P1: track entries and exits mark chapter boundaries; pulling back ~30 s before a section end creates anticipation.
- P2: design cues for narrative roles (question / investigation / human cost / antagonist / reveal / coda), not per-scene cues.
- P3: "score documentary structure, not seconds" — motifs mark function (investigation, weight, transition).

**Rule:** every cue placement has a written one-sentence justification in project notes. If we can't write the sentence, the cue doesn't ship.

### 1.4 Voice is sovereign
- P1: every channel surveyed treats narration intelligibility as the non-negotiable top priority.
- P2: music bed sits 9–12 LU below VO when speaking; the only foreground music is in deliberately VO-absent passages.
- P3: implicit — "the most important factual claim plays dry."

**Rule:** never let music compete with a load-bearing sentence. Captions-on viewers (a real chunk of the YouTube audience) get nothing from a bed but everything from a clean visual.

### 1.5 Restraint reads as honesty; earn the swell
- P1: warn against heart-stirring music before the story has earned it.
- P2: Plainly Difficult's restraint on disaster material is the model for any topic with real consequence; bigger swells require more honest facts to back them up.
- P3: only the closing card lets music tell the audience how to feel — by then the facts have been delivered and the score is reflecting, not coloring.

**Rule:** open with neutral curiosity, not heart-on-sleeve emotion. Reserve harmonic/dynamic payoff for the final coda.

## 2. Points of disagreement and how I'm resolving them

### 2.1 Primary sourcing tier — DISAGREEMENT, RESOLVED IN FAVOR OF P3
- P1: library music is fine if sliced like Joss Fong (8-bar phrase boundaries, splice endings into earlier sections).
- P2: pick one tier and commit — commissioned custom score (~$1.5–4k for ~6 cues + 3 stinger variations) **or** discipline yourself with a single subscription library (Epidemic Sound default). The "random royalty-free middle" only works for comedy.
- P3: reject generic royalty-free libraries entirely. Source order: (1) period public-domain → (2) original/AI-generated → (3) named CC-BY artists.

**Resolution.** P3 wins for this project. The Mark Hanna documentary is set in 1896, runs short-form, and has no cash budget — period-public-domain music (1890s march, parlor, ragtime, Sousa, Joplin) simultaneously solves the budget, the licensing, and the authenticity question. P2's "commit to one tier" rule still holds; we're committing to the period-PD tier rather than a subscription library. P1's library-slicing craft is preserved but applied to PD recordings rather than Epidemic Sound cues. We do **not** sample multiple subscription libraries.

### 2.2 Cue count and rotation rate — RESOLVED BY RUNTIME
- P1: change cues every 20–40 s during dense exposition (Vox / Joss Fong cadence).
- P2: 4–8 cues across a 15–30 min video; cue recall preferred.
- P3: only two leitmotifs total for the whole short-form film.

**Resolution.** P1's 20–40 s rotation is the explainer-doc model (Vox); it does not apply to historical-doc register. For our short-form (single-digit-minutes) cut, P3's two leitmotifs is the right ceiling. If the cut grows past ~10 min, add a third cue (typically a thinner reharmonization of the opening for the coda). Never exceed four named cues in a piece this short.

### 2.3 Ducking depth — RESOLVED BY CUE DENSITY
- P1: −12 to −15 dB baseline + breath swells +2–4 dB in inter-sentence gaps.
- P2: −5 to −8 dB (wider than tutorials' −3 dB but narrower than P1).
- P3: doesn't specify (because the main answer is "less music").

**Resolution.** Both P1 and P2 are right at different cue densities. Use:
- −8 dB duck for sparse pad/drone/single-piano cues (LEMMiNO/Plainly Difficult register). Wider duck would over-suppress already-thin material. This is the default everywhere because §3.2 forbids dense cues.
- −12 dB duck is reserved for the title-card statement of Cue A only, when the snare-ostinato is allowed to play as a fuller arrangement. Wider duck because mid-range content masks consonants.
- Add a manual breath-swell automation of +2–4 dB in inter-sentence gaps during emotionally weighted passages (P1's "music wants to surface" trick). Keep flat ducking for explanatory passages where unbroken voice clarity matters.

### 2.4 Loudness target — ADOPT P2 (only concrete numbers offered)
- P1: doesn't specify.
- P2: master integrated −14 LUFS; voice short-term −16 LUFS; music bed −27 to −28 LUFS short-term when VO is present (~12 LU below voice); music ducks back to −20 LUFS in pure-B-roll moments. None of P2's reference channels exceeds −10 LUFS integrated (YouTube normalization caps it anyway).
- P3: doesn't specify.

**Resolution.** Use P2's numbers verbatim. They're the only concrete spec on the table and they match YouTube's normalization sweet spot.

### 2.5 AI-generated music (Suno/Udio) — RESOLVED BY DISCIPLINE
- P1: not mentioned.
- P2: flags Internet Historian's recent Suno cues as a wildcard with unsettled licensing.
- P3: explicitly endorses AI generation as the second-tier source, with a hard logging requirement (prompt, model, generation date, paid-tier TOS screenshot the day of generation).

**Resolution.** P3's logging discipline answers P2's concern. AI-generated cues are allowed for original motifs only (not full-cue replacements), only on a paid tier where commercial rights are explicit, and every cue must be entered into `assets/MUSIC_LICENSES.md` with prompt + model + date + license terms. AI cues never replace dry voice on the most important sentence; they live in the same restrained role as the period-PD cues.

### 2.6 Library music slicing technique — RETAINED EVEN THOUGH WE'RE NOT USING LIBRARIES
P1's Joss Fong technique (8-bar phrase boundaries, splice ending sections into earlier positions, change cues at chapter shifts only) **does not apply** to our period-PD sources directly because period march/parlor pieces have stricter phrase structure already. But the principle — **never cut mid-phrase, only at musical phrase boundaries, prefer crossfades near zero crossings before transients** — is universal and we adopt it.

## 3. Final music plan — Mark Hanna / 1896 documentary

### 3.1 Sourcing strategy
1. **Primary: 1890s period public-domain recordings.** Audition order:
   - Library of Congress *National Jukebox* (https://www.loc.gov/collections/national-jukebox/) — earliest commercial recordings, many 1900s but some 1890s; tone-period-correct.
   - IMSLP (https://imslp.org) for Sousa marches, Joplin rags, period band scores. Use the *recording* license, not just the score license.
   - Musopen (https://musopen.org) for PD classical recordings.
   - Internet Archive (https://archive.org) for transferred wax-cylinder and early-78 recordings of period band/parlor music.
2. **Secondary: original AI-generated instrumental motifs.** Suno (paid Pro tier) or Udio (paid tier). Mandatory prompt scaffold: *"1890s [instrument family], sparse, instrumental, no vocals, public-domain feel, [mood]"*. Every cue logged in `assets/MUSIC_LICENSES.md` with prompt + model + generation date + screenshot of paid-tier TOS for that day.
3. **Tertiary fallback: named CC-BY artists** from Free Music Archive or Pixabay's *named composer* uploads (not the anonymous keyword-stuffed kind). Always credited in the YouTube description.
4. **Forbidden:**
   - Generic royalty-free libraries (Epidemic Sound, Artlist, Soundstripe, Musicbed). They date the work and audibly belong to corporate explainers, not historical docs. (this is the policy choice that distinguishes this plan from every other YouTube doc plan; see §2.1 resolution)
   - Any cue without all four logged provenance fields (source URL, license text, attribution string, date downloaded).
   - Commercial recordings under any "fair use" theory. Fair use is a legal defense, not a release valve.
5. **Period window:** 1890–1898 inclusive is strict period; 1899–1902 is post-campaign and acceptable only as flavor texture for Cue B or sting variants — never as Cue A "Mechanism" base material.

### 3.2 Cue palette (genre/mood per segment)
Two leitmotifs anchor the score. A third "coda" motif is a reharmonization of motif A.

| Cue | Function | Texture | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — "Mechanism"** | Forward motion; introducing a structural fact about the 1896 campaign (fundraising machinery, surrogate networks, coercion of immigrant labor, etc.). | Period-correct rhythmic figure: sparse military-march snare ostinato (default — matches DESIGN.md "Decisive, not bouncy"); parlor piano with subtle low-string drone as fallback if the snare-ostinato register fights the visual cut. Mid-tempo. No melodic peak. | Title card (full statement, ~6–8 s, then ducks); section starts when naming a campaign mechanism. **Exits before** the section's climax sentence — that sentence plays dry. |
| **B — "Weight"** | Human cost; consequence; what the mechanism *did* to people. | Single-line minor-mode melody on solo piano OR muted cornet. No accompaniment. Slow, breath-paced. | Beats where the doc names a person, a wage figure, a vote count, a death. Enter quietly behind B-roll, exit on the cut to next narrator phrase. |
| **C — "Coda"** | Reflective close. The only place music tells the audience how to feel. | Motif A's contour, reharmonized into Motif B's minor-mode register, full volume, slow. | Closing card only. Plays out over static end card; fade to silence. (audition both full-volume and thinned-reharmonization variants — see §4) |
| **Sting (chapter transitions)** | 1–2 s motif sting between sections; never a fade-out/fade-in. | Pick **one** stinger sound and use it for every chapter transition. Default: low-piano-and-sub. Alternate: a single muted-cornet hit. Mixing stinger families is the fastest way to make the doc sound amateur. | All inter-section transitions; same family throughout the piece. |

**Indictment beats are deliberately unscored.** The narration is structurally three-part — mechanism description → human cost → indictment. Mechanism = Cue A; human cost = Cue B; indictment plays in dry voice + room tone. This is not a missing fourth cue; the absence of a cue is the cue.

### 3.3 Structural placement (score the structure, not the seconds)
- **Cold open (0:00–0:15):** silence or single sustained low drone. No melody. Lets the first caption land.
- **Title card:** Cue A states itself in full for ~6–8 s, then ducks under narration.
- **Body sections:** Cue A enters at section start, exits before the section's most important sentence (which plays dry). Cue B enters quietly under human-cost B-roll. Inter-section transitions get a 1–2 s motif sting from the same family — never a fade-out/fade-in.
- **The single biggest reveal:** full music drop-out 1–2 s before the line. Line plays in dry voice + room tone. Re-enter on a single low piano note (transformed motif A). This earns the silence rule (Section 1.1) at maximum impact.
- **Closing:** Cue C at full volume over a static end card. Fade to silence. (audition both full-volume and thinned-reharmonization variants — see §4)

Target: **40–60% of total runtime unscored** (P3's rule, supported by P1/P2 silence-as-cue convergence).

### 3.4 Mixing guidelines (concrete numbers)
- **Master integrated:** −14 LUFS (YouTube normalization sweet spot).
- **Voice short-term:** −16 LUFS.
- **Music bed when VO present:** −27 to −28 LUFS short-term (~12 LU below voice).
- **Music in pure-B-roll / VO-absent moments:** ducks back up to −20 LUFS.
- **Duck depth (auto-duck baseline):** −8 dB for sparse pad/drone/solo-piano cues (the default — §3.2 forbids dense cues). −12 dB is reserved for the title-card statement of Cue A only, when the snare-ostinato plays as a fuller arrangement.
- **Manual breath swells:** +2–4 dB in inter-sentence gaps during emotionally weighted passages so music feels like it "wants to surface." Flat ducking for explanatory passages where unbroken voice clarity matters.
- **EQ pocket on music bus:** carve −3 to −4 dB at 1–4 kHz to clear the vocal band. Premiere's "Make Room for Vocals" preset or equivalent on DaVinci.
- **Voice EQ:** HPF at ~80–100 Hz; modest 2–4 kHz presence boost.
- **Sting family:** see §3.2 cue palette (the sting row defines the chosen family; this is a cue-palette decision, not a mixing tweak).
- **Edits:** cut at musical phrase boundaries, never mid-phrase. Crossfades in quiet spots, near zero crossings, before transients.

### 3.5 Concrete shortlist to audition
Ordered roughly by expected fit. The editor should pull the first 3–5 hits from each row and audition against the locked cut.

| Source | Search prompt / catalog query | Likely role |
|---|---|---|
| Library of Congress National Jukebox | "1896 march band", "1898 parlor piano", "Sousa Stars and Stripes Forever" (1896), "Sousa Liberty Bell" (1893) | Cue A "Mechanism" base material |
| IMSLP (recordings tab only) | Joplin "Maple Leaf Rag" (1899; period-adjacent — flavor only, not Cue A), Sousa marches in PD recording, period band arrangements of campaign songs | Cue A variation, optional title-card flourish |

The editor will produce a 30-second audition reel per cue role and review against the locked cut before committing.

### 3.6 Copyright and provenance protocol (binary)
- Every cue logged in `assets/MUSIC_LICENSES.md` with: source URL, license text quoted, attribution string for the YouTube description, date downloaded. Missing any field → cue does not ship.
- AI-generated cues additionally log: prompt, model name, generation date, screenshot of the paid-tier TOS as of generation date.
- Final cut uploaded as **unlisted** on YouTube; wait for the Content ID scan to complete before publishing publicly. Cheap and dispositive against accidental matches.
- If a Content ID claim arrives during the unlisted scan, **dispute** with the LoC/IMSLP/Archive source URL plus the license text already logged in `assets/MUSIC_LICENSES.md`. Do **not** swap the cue first — the dispute is the test of whether the provenance log is real. Cue swap is only correct if the dispute is rejected on appeal.
- One Content ID claim on a documentary of this register breaks viewer trust. The downside is asymmetric; there is no upside to cutting corners on this.

## 4. Confidence section (where reviewers converged vs diverged)

### HIGH confidence (3 of 3 reviewers explicitly converge)
- Silence as a structural tool / aim for substantial unscored runtime.
- Motif reuse over cue variety; recurring motifs do narrative work for free.
- Score function (narrative role), not timecode.
- Voice is sovereign; never let music compete with a load-bearing sentence.
- Earn the emotional swell; restraint reads as honesty on hard material.

### MEDIUM confidence (2 of 3, or one reviewer provides specifics the others don't contradict)
- Concrete loudness numbers (−14 LUFS integrated, voice −16 LUFS short-term, bed −27 to −28 LUFS): only P2 specifies; P1/P3 don't disagree, just don't quantify. Adopted because they match YouTube norms.
- Single sting family for transitions: P2 explicit, P1 implicit (beat alignment), P3 compatible (motif sting at chapter transitions).
- AI-generated music is acceptable with strict logging: P3 explicit, P2 raises caveat that P3's protocol answers, P1 silent.
- Breath ducking (+2–4 dB swells in inter-sentence gaps): P1 explicit, P2/P3 silent. Adopted because it's specific and reversible.

### LOW confidence (genuine disagreement, resolved by project context — not by averaging) *(scope assumptions verified by OPINION.md 2026-06-06; do not re-litigate)*
- **Primary sourcing tier.** P1 (library OK if sliced well) vs P3 (period-PD only, reject generic royalty-free) vs P2 (commit to one tier — either commissioned or single library). Resolved in favor of P3 because the Mark Hanna 1896 context makes period-PD strictly dominant on cost, license, and authenticity. **If the project changes** (e.g. this becomes a contemporary explainer, or budget appears for a commissioned composer), revisit this decision.
- **Cue rotation rate.** P1 (20–40 s) is for the explainer-doc register and explicitly does not apply here. P3's two-leitmotif ceiling holds for short-form. Recorded as scale-dependent rather than a flat disagreement.
- **Duck depth.** P1 (−12 to −15 dB) and P2 (−5 to −8 dB) differ by ~5 dB. Resolved by cue density rather than by averaging — both are right at different texture choices.
