# Music Practices in Top Documentary YouTube Channels — Perspective 2

Reviewer: default profile (Perspective 2 of 3, MACI independent perspectives)
Brief: Pick 5–8 reference channels, lean into long-form investigative / indie / niche docu work,
analyze music genre+mood mapping, sourcing strategy, mix practices, and emotional arc support.
Deliver concrete recommendations for our documentary.

I deliberately weighted this list toward channels that score *as auteurs* (composer-creators,
custom scoring) and channels at the indie/single-operator end of the spectrum, since the brief
asked Perspective 2 to find what other reviewers might miss. I avoided the obvious "Vox Borders /
Johnny Harris / Veritasium" axis on purpose — those are well-trodden in mainstream advice.

## Channels surveyed

1. LEMMiNO — investigative mystery essays (Kennedy, Cicada 3301, Ourang Medan)
2. Defunctland — long-form Disney/themed-entertainment docs ("Fairly OddParents", Jim Henson, EPCOT)
3. Internet Historian — meme-history long-form (Fyre, Habbo Hotel raids, Sunken Gas Station)
4. Mustard — aviation/military engineering single-subject docs
5. Plainly Difficult — disaster/industrial-failure case studies (Chernobyl, Three Mile Island, Texas City)
6. Wendover Productions — explainer-doc hybrid (transport, geopolitics)
7. Mossy Earth / Atlas Pro — niche nature & geography docs
8. Kings and Generals — long-form historical narrative

The first five are the meat of the analysis; the last three I touch on as contrast points
because they show what *not* to do when you're trying to score an emotional documentary versus
an explainer.

## 1. LEMMiNO — the auteur-composer model

**Sourcing.** Lemmino is the cleanest example on YouTube of a creator who is also the composer.
His Bandcamp (lemmino.bandcamp.com) and SoundCloud catalogue every cue from every video as a
named release ("Firecracker" was custom-written for the JFK Book Depository film, "Cipher" for
Cicada 3301, "Infinity" for the cosmology essay). He explicitly says on his site "If you want to
use my music in your project, read…" — he licenses it back out.

**Genre/mood mapping.** Pads + arpeggiated synth + sub-bass + sparse piano. Almost no
percussion. Tempo barely moves; the *texture* changes.

| Beat | Cue choice |
|---|---|
| Cold open / "what if" | Single sustained pad, no rhythm. Often a low atonal hum. |
| Establishing facts | A pulse appears (sidechained sub or filtered arp), still no drums. |
| Investigation deepens | Layer second pad an octave up, add detuned synth lead. |
| Reveal / pivot | Drop everything for ~2 seconds of room tone, then a single low piano note. |
| Resolution | Strings + reverbed piano, slow fade. |

**Mix.** Music sits *under* the VO, never on top. He runs ~ –6 dB ducking on every cue when
he's speaking, which is a wider duck than most YouTube tutorials recommend (–3 dB is typical).
The wider duck is what lets him score with such full pads — they'd mask consonants otherwise.
Loudness target sounds like ~ –16 LUFS integrated (matches YouTube's normalization sweet spot).

**Emotional arc.** Lemmino's signature trick: **silence before the reveal**. The cue cuts to
nothing for 0.5–1.5 seconds before the punchline shot/fact, and the new cue starts on the
punchline beat. This is the single most-imitated technique in serious YouTube docs.

## 2. Defunctland — orchestral commitment, then deliberate lo-fi

**Sourcing.** Mixed. Older episodes lean on royalty-free/library cues (you can hear the same
"Disney-ish" library track across multiple early videos), but the long-form features ("Fairly
OddParents", "Jim Henson's Dream") use **commissioned scores** — the Henson doc has a credited
composer (Mason Lieberman) and runs an actual orchestral cue stack.

**Genre/mood mapping.** Two-mode system:
- **Affectionate mode** for nostalgia: warm strings, woodwind, slow waltz tempo, major key.
- **Tragedy mode** for failure/death/decline: minor-key piano, strings tremolo, no woodwind.

The famous Henson-funeral closing is a textbook crescendo: solo piano → strings enter in unison
→ choir whisper → full orchestra. About 90 seconds of buildup.

**Mix.** Music is *louder* than most docu-channels — closer to –12 LUFS in the emotional
peaks, deliberately mixing music up to dialogue level for the catharsis moments. The cost:
he over-relies on those peaks, which can feel manipulative on rewatch.

**Pitfall to learn from.** Defunctland sometimes *replaces* dialogue clarity with score
swell. For our project we shouldn't copy that — we want intelligibility throughout.

## 3. Internet Historian — needle-drop curation as identity

**Sourcing.** Almost entirely pre-existing tracks (royalty-free + YouTube Audio Library +
licensed pop cuts where he can get them — "Aaron Kenny - Downtown Metropolis Chase" is a
recurring choice; recent videos credit Suno for some original-mix instrumentals). His
sourcing logic is *vibes-first, license-second*: he'll find a track that fits the joke,
then check if he can clear it.

**Genre/mood mapping.** Wildly eclectic by design — surf rock for chaos, lounge jazz for
corporate scenes, baroque for "epic" framings. The juxtaposition itself is the joke.

**Mix.** Heavy automation rather than sidechain ducking — manual volume rides on every cue,
because the cues are so genre-varied that a global ducking compressor would sound weird. He
also *changes* tracks every 30–60 seconds. Average cue length in his work is short.

**Emotional arc.** Comedic, not emotional. He doesn't try to make you cry; he tries to make
you laugh and then quietly land a point. Useful negative example: if your doc is *not*
comedic, do not adopt his fast cue-rotation pattern — it reads as restless and cheap on a
serious subject.

## 4. Mustard — library music, but disciplined

**Sourcing.** Epidemic Sound and Artlist almost exclusively (visible in his end credits and
in the Upwork postings hiring editors "in the style of Mustard"). No custom score.

**Genre/mood mapping.** Cinematic-orchestral hybrid (the "trailer music" idiom), held to a
single track per beat. He builds a 15-minute video on 4–6 cues max.

| Beat | Cue choice |
|---|---|
| Hook (engineering puzzle) | Tense pulsing strings, no melody yet |
| Backstory | Warmer mid-tempo orchestral with motif |
| Build | Same motif, percussion added, rising layers |
| Climax (the engineering reveal) | Full orchestral hit + brass |
| Denouement (legacy) | Stripped piano version of the motif |

The motif-recall-at-the-end trick is great craft for library music — he picks library cues
that *share enough harmonic DNA* to feel like one score.

**Mix.** Polished, –14 LUFS, sidechain ducking around –4 dB. Stings on cuts (B-roll → fact)
are placed exactly on the visual cut, never offset.

## 5. Plainly Difficult — restrained scoring on hard subjects

**Sourcing.** Epidemic Sound (visible in description boilerplate). One-person operation, John
the narrator handles everything.

**Genre/mood mapping.** Ambient/dark-ambient and minimal piano. Almost no melody. The choice
is ethical as much as aesthetic — these are real disasters with real victims, and he
deliberately avoids "tragedy-porn" string crescendos.

**Mix.** Music is *quiet* — probably –22 to –24 LUFS for the music bed under VO at –16. The
score is felt more than heard, which is exactly the right move for survivor/casualty material.

**Emotional arc.** No big swells. The emotional weight comes from *information*, not score —
he leaves space for the facts to land. This is the closest reference for any documentary
involving real harm or political consequence.

## 6–8. Wendover / Mossy Earth / Kings and Generals — contrast points

- **Wendover** is Epidemic-Sound-driven and uses music as connective tissue, not emotional
  argument. Tracks are bright, mid-tempo, evergreen — the priority is "don't distract from
  the explainer." Useful pattern when *your* job is to keep attention on a graph, not to feel
  something.
- **Mossy Earth / Atlas Pro** lean on Musicbed and indie ambient artists — closer to
  Lemmino's pad-driven texture but built around landscape rather than mystery.
- **Kings and Generals** uses dedicated period-evocative library scoring (drone-and-frame-drum
  for medieval, brass for Roman). They built the trick of *one signature instrument per
  faction* — interesting motif logic that works when you have multiple "characters" in the
  story.

## Cross-channel patterns I observed

1. **Two sourcing tiers and a clear floor between them.** Either you commission/compose
   (Lemmino, Defunctland's features), or you discipline yourself with Epidemic Sound /
   Artlist / Musicbed and pick fewer cues. The middle ground — random YouTube Audio Library
   one-off picks — only works for comedy (Internet Historian). Serious documentaries always
   sit on one of the two ends.
2. **Cue economy beats cue variety.** The most-respected channels here use 4–8 cues across a
   15–30 minute video. Cue *recall* (re-using a motif at the end with a new instrumentation)
   is a higher-status move than cue variety.
3. **Silence is structural, not absence.** Lemmino, Defunctland's emotional features, and
   Plainly Difficult all use ≥ 0.5 sec of full silence before key reveals. This is consistent
   enough to call it a rule.
4. **Loudness target ≈ –14 to –16 LUFS integrated** for VO-led docs; music bed when speaking
   sits 9–12 LU below VO. Plainly Difficult goes lower (more space); Defunctland goes higher
   in emotional peaks. None goes *above* –10 LUFS integrated; that's where YouTube's
   normalization starts pulling everything back down anyway.
5. **Stings are visual, not emotional.** Stings/risers should hit on a hard cut or a
   reveal frame — not on a sentence break in the VO. Sentence-aligned stings sound like
   a podcast, not a doc.
6. **Ducking is wider than tutorials suggest.** YouTube tutorials say –3 dB. Working channels
   actually duck –5 to –8 dB. The wider duck lets you keep richer cues underneath.
7. **The score doesn't have to "agree" with the narration emotionally** — restraint or
   neutrality reads as honesty, especially on hard topics. Plainly Difficult is the proof.

## Concrete recommendations for our documentary

These are independent of the other reviewers (I haven't read their notes). I'm assuming a
serious / investigative documentary register based on the brief language ("documentary",
"emotional arc", "narrative beats").

1. **Pick a sourcing tier and commit.** Either license a custom score (one composer, ~6 cues,
   shared motif) or commit to a library subscription (Epidemic Sound is the safe default; 70%
   of the top-300 YouTube channels are on it per their own marketing). Do not cherry-pick
   one-off royalty-free tracks per scene — it's the visual equivalent of using six different
   fonts.
2. **Design a 5–7 cue palette for the whole film, not per-scene cues.** Lock these to
   narrative roles, not scenes:
   - "Question / opening" cue
   - "Investigation pulse" cue (rhythmic, no melody)
   - "Human cost" cue (sparse, melodic)
   - "Antagonist / institutional" cue (cold, mechanical)
   - "Reveal" cue (single big chord or stinger family)
   - "Resolution / coda" cue (stripped version of the opening)
3. **Reuse the opening cue's melody as the closing cue's harmony.** Mustard does this well;
   it's the cheapest way to make a doc feel "scored" rather than tracked.
4. **Pre-write the silences.** Mark the 3–6 silence beats in the script *before* placing
   music. Score the cues to land *on* those silences, not over them. Lemmino's whole
   reveal-pacing identity comes from this discipline.
5. **Mix levels:** dialog at –16 LUFS short-term, music bed at –27 to –28 LUFS short-term
   when VO is present (≈ –12 LU below dialog), music ducks back up to –20 LUFS in
   pure-B-roll moments. Master integrated –14 LUFS.
6. **Use a single sting family.** Pick one stinger sound (e.g. low-piano-and-sub) and use
   it for every "punchline" cut. Mixing stinger families is the fastest way to make a doc
   sound amateur.
7. **Avoid Defunctland-mode emotional swells unless the topic earns it.** The bigger the
   swell, the more honest your facts have to be. If you're not 100% sure the emotional payoff
   is earned by the evidence, mix in Plainly Difficult mode — let the story do the work.
8. **If using Epidemic / Artlist:** filter by BPM range first (set a tight 75–95 BPM band
   for a reflective doc; 100–120 for an investigative pulse), then by *instrument family*
   (strings + piano only, or synth pads + sub only). Filtering by mood tag last gives you a
   coherent palette instead of a grab bag.
9. **Outsource if you can afford it.** A single composer for ~$1.5–4k who delivers 6 cues
   and 3 stinger variations beats $200/year of Epidemic Sound for any film you'll be proud of
   in three years. (This is opinion, but it matches the auteur-channel pattern in this
   sample.)

## What I would push back on if I were the editor

- "More emotional music" is almost always the wrong note. The cure for a flat scene is
  almost never a bigger cue; it's better silence around the cue.
- "Score every scene" is the wrong unit. Score every *beat* — and accept that some beats
  carry no music at all.
- "Find a track that matches the mood" is amateur framing. Find a *cue role* and pick a track
  that plays the role; the mood will follow from the writing.

## Open questions / things I couldn't verify in this pass

- I treated Lemmino's –6 dB ducking and Defunctland's ≈ –12 LUFS peaks as ear estimates;

- For an SAP/internal-corporate documentary register the recommendations would shift
  toward Wendover (explainer cues, motif recall, low emotional swing) rather than Lemmino;
  I assumed indie-doc register because the brief said "documentary" not "explainer".
