✅ **New Lisbon (now Lisbon), Columbiana County, Ohio.**

## REMAINING TO RESEARCH (Round 2)

- [x] **Hanna's Senate role** — NOT chair of Senate Republican Conference. His real power base was **RNC chairman, 1896-1904**. Senate committee chairs: Relations with Canada (55th-57th Cong, ~1897-1903), Enrolled Bills (57th Cong), **Interoceanic Canals (58th Cong, 1903-05)** — colleagues nicknamed Panama route "Hannama Canal." Source: Library of Congress authority record n50082280.

- [x] **National Civic Federation** — ✅ Hanna was **first president of the NCF**, founded 1900. Chair of permanent board of conciliation and arbitration (Dec 1901). In **1902 anthracite coal strike**, Hanna persuaded coal operators to negotiate; assisted Roosevelt's mediation that resolved the strike in October 1902. Source: Britannica; Theodore Roosevelt Center.

- [x] **McKinley's actual policy record (what the 1896 ledger purchased):**
  - **Dingley Tariff Act, 1897**: avg ~49% on dutiable imports, "highest protective tariff in US history to that time." Wool reached ~91%. Some items rose 57% over previous law. Sponsor: Rep. Nelson Dingley (R-ME, House Ways & Means).
  - **Gold Standard Act, March 14, 1900**: formally placed US currency on gold; ended bimetallic ambiguity; rejected free silver. Fulfilled the 1896 Republican platform pledge.
  - **Spanish-American War, April 1898**: 10-week war. Treaty of Paris (Dec 1898/Feb 1899): US acquired **Puerto Rico, Guam, Philippines** (paid Spain $20M for Philippines); Cuba relinquished by Spain, under US military occupation until 1902 (later Platt Amendment).
  - **Hawaii annexation, July 7, 1898**: Newlands Resolution (joint congressional resolution; not a treaty). Hawaii became Territory of Hawaii in 1900.
  - **Open Door Policy, 1899-1900**: free commercial access to China, equal opportunity for all powers.
  - Sources: Miller Center; Wikipedia (Presidency of William McKinley); Britannica.

- [x] **Bryan after 1896:**
  - **1900**: lost to McKinley. ~6.36M popular votes (45.5%), **155 electoral votes**.
  - **1908**: lost to Taft. ~6.41M popular votes (~43%), **162 electoral votes**.
  - Three-time loser. Helped Wilson get 1912 nomination → **Secretary of State 1913-1915 under Wilson**. Resigned June 1915 over Wilson's Lusitania response (he was an avowed pacifist).
  - **Died July 26, 1925**, in Dayton, Tennessee — five days after the Scopes Trial concluded (he was on the prosecution team).
  - Sources: Britannica; 270toWin; Wisconsin Historical Society.

- [x] **🚨 Hanna's "madman" quote — UNRELIABLE:**
  - "Don't any of you realize there's only one life between this madman and the presidency?" — earliest documented source is **Thomas Beer's *Hanna* (1929)**.
  - **Beer's Hanna documents were forensically analyzed by Carole E. Chaski (2013) — at least some are inauthentic or fabricated.** Beer is now suspect.
  - Croly 1912 does NOT contain this line. No contemporary 1900 source documents it.
  - **Standard scholarly treatment: anecdotal at best, possibly apocryphal.** If used in v4: must be flagged "as Beer reported it" or cut.
  - Sources: History News Network on Chaski's forensic work; Theodore Roosevelt Center.

- [x] **🟢 "That damned cowboy is President of the United States" — SAFER:**
  - Source: **Hermann H. Kohlsaat, *From McKinley to Harding* (1923)** — Kohlsaat was on the 1893 syndicate, knew Hanna directly. This is the better-attested Hanna quote and dates to September 1901 (after McKinley assassination).
  - Source: Library of Congress "Today in History"; LibQuotes citing Kohlsaat 1923.

## REMAINING TO RESEARCH (Round 3)

- [x] **🟢 James Benjamin Parker** — Atlanta-born (1857), African American, formerly enslaved. Multiple jobs across his life (newspaper salesman, Savannah constable, Atlanta letter carrier, waiter). On Sept 6 1901 he was a **recently laid-off waiter from the Plaza Restaurant at the Pan-American Exposition**, in the receiving line as private citizen. Atlanta Constitution Sept 8 1901 headlined "Assassin Was Caught By an Atlanta Man." Six-foot-six. Tackled Czolgosz. Source: Wikipedia James Benjamin Parker; *Journal of the Gilded Age and Progressive Era* 2002 article on "Big Jim" Parker; Buffalo math.buffalo.edu history.

- [⚠️] **Hanna's death location** — perplexity could NOT settle Lafayette Square home vs Arlington Hotel definitively. Both are mentioned in different secondary sources. Wikipedia and Senate Featured Bios confirm Washington DC + Feb 15 1904 + typhoid fever, but not the exact building. **For v4: keep generic — "in Washington" or "in his Washington residence" — do NOT claim Lafayette Square OR Arlington Hotel specifically.**

- [⚠️] **Hanna funeral procession length** — perplexity could NOT verify the "three miles" claim from the sources searched. What IS documented: funeral services were held in the **U.S. Senate Chamber** (an extraordinary honor); interment at **Lake View Cemetery, Cleveland**. **For v4: cut the three-mile claim. Replace with the Senate-Chamber + Lake View Cemetery facts.**

- [x] **🚨 "Permanent Republican majority" — NOT Hanna 1896**:
  - The phrase entered modern political journalism in **April 2001** (Time magazine reference cited by *American Prospect*). Karl Rove himself later denied saying "permanent" — he claimed he said "durable."
  - Lemann's "The Controller" (New Yorker May 12, 2003) framed Rove as following in Hanna's *spirit*, but did NOT quote Hanna using the exact phrase. The phrase is a 21st-century journalistic coinage attached to Rove.
  - **For v4: do NOT claim "permanent Republican majority" was Hanna's phrase from 1896. Reframe: "Karl Rove studied Hanna; journalists later put the phrase 'permanent Republican majority' on Rove's project."**
  - Source: Metafilter research thread; American Prospect retrospective; Wiley publication on Rove.

- [x] **🟢 McKinley's documented last words**:
  - Most-documented version: **"Good-bye, all; good-bye. It is God's way. His will be done."** Source: Dr. Matthew D. Mann, attending surgeon, wrote it down at the bedside. American Monthly Review of Reviews vol 24 no 4.
  - Earlier in the death scene, McKinley **softly sang/recited "Nearer, My God, to Thee"** with Ida at his side. Multiple primary medical accounts confirm this.
  - Died at **Milburn House, 1168 Delaware Avenue, Buffalo**, at 2:15 AM Sept 14 1901.
  - Cabinet present except Secretary of State and Treasury. Sources: McKinley Assassination Ink; Miller Center; Wikipedia.

- [⚠️] **Hanna's last words / death scene detail** — perplexity could NOT find documented last words. Standard historical references don't preserve a specific quotation. **For v4: do not invent. Hanna died of typhoid fever Feb 15 1904 in Washington DC at age 66, full stop, no fabricated last words.** Cause of death (typhoid fever) is confirmed.
