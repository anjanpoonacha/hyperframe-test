And in eighteen ninety-six, he did something no one had ever done at that scale.

He turned the American presidential election into a corporate purchase order.

Three and a half million dollars raised. Twenty times what his opponent spent.

Banks assessed at a quarter of a percent of capital. Railroads, shaken down personally. Standard Oil, two hundred and fifty thousand dollars. JP Morgan, two hundred and fifty thousand dollars.

Half a million voters, transported by train, to shake one man's hand.

Two hundred million pieces of campaign literature, printed and mailed.

The opposition had a thirty-six year old preacher who could move a crowd to tears.

Hanna had a ledger.

And when November came, the ledger won.

To understand how — we go back fifty-nine years.
